# ASTRAVEN — Project Vision

## 1. Project Identity

**Working Title:** ASTRAVEN

**Genre:**
2D Side-Scrolling Action Adventure / Run & Gun / Sci-Fi Exploration

**Core Tone:**
- Japanese Retro Sci-Fi
- Cosmic Mystery
- Exploration
- Isolation
- Survival
- Light Horror
- Adventure first, horror second

ASTRAVEN should feel mysterious and unsettling, but not constantly terrifying.

The player should feel curious enough to move forward.

---

## 2. Core Fantasy

The player is a space explorer sent to investigate a strange signal.

What begins as a routine expedition becomes a journey through:

- abandoned space stations
- deserted colonies
- alien planets
- forgotten research facilities
- lost ships
- ruined human settlements

The player slowly discovers that alien lifeforms have spread across multiple worlds.

Human civilization may have already collapsed.

The long-term goal becomes:

**survive, investigate, and search for remaining survivors.**

---

## 3. Core Experience

The game should combine:

### Action
Fast and satisfying gunplay.

### Exploration
Players should want to see what is beyond the next room or landscape.

### Atmosphere
Sound, lighting, silence, and environmental storytelling are extremely important.

### Mystery
The player should constantly feel:

> "Something happened here."

and later:

> "How far has this spread?"

---

## 4. Gameplay Style

Main gameplay:

- Move
- Run
- Jump
- Shoot
- Dodge / Roll
- Interact
- Explore
- Fight enemies
- Discover environmental clues

Possible future mechanics:

- Melee
- Dash
- Parry
- Special movement
- Environmental hazards

Do not add future mechanics until the basic combat and movement feel good.

---

## 5. Combat Philosophy

Combat must feel powerful and physical.

Important feedback:

- strong gun sounds
- recoil
- muzzle flash
- shell casings
- hit reaction
- impact particles
- smoke
- explosions
- subtle camera shake

The player should enjoy shooting even before advanced systems exist.

---

## 6. Weapons

Weapons should primarily be inspired by real-world firearms and gunpowder-based weapon systems.

Style:

**Realistic Sci-Fi Firearms**

Main weapon categories may include:

- Assault Rifle
- Battle Rifle
- SMG / PDW
- Shotgun
- LMG
- Precision Rifle
- Pistol
- Heavy Pistol / Revolver

Future technology should mainly appear through:

- advanced materials
- optics
- targeting systems
- smart ammunition
- weapon attachments
- improved recoil systems

Energy weapons, plasma weapons, laser weapons, and alien weapons should be rare and special.

They should feel unusual when discovered.

---

## 7. Art Direction

Visual style:

- 2D Pixel Art
- Japanese Retro Sci-Fi influence
- detailed environments
- soft but atmospheric color palette
- readable silhouettes
- cinematic lighting
- strong depth using layered backgrounds

The visual tone should be softer than pure horror.

The player should see:

- beautiful alien skies
- strange planets
- distant moons
- ruined structures
- abandoned stations
- mysterious landscapes

The world should feel dangerous but also beautiful.

---

## 8. Environment Structure

Scenes should use multiple visual layers:

1. Far background
2. Mid background
3. Gameplay layer
4. Foreground
5. Atmospheric effects

Use:

- Parallax
- Fog
- Dust
- Smoke
- Flickering lights
- Environmental particles
- Moving machinery
- Background creatures or silhouettes

Do not build entire levels as one large image.

Prefer modular assets and reusable environment kits.

---

## 9. UI

UI should be:

- Minimal
- Futuristic
- Easy to read
- Not cluttered

The game language is primarily:

**Thai**

Use Thai pixel-style fonts or Thai fonts that visually match retro pixel art.

Important UI information:

- Health
- Energy / Stamina if needed
- Ammo
- Grenades
- Objective
- Map
- Interaction prompts

Avoid unnecessary HUD elements.

---

## 10. Sound Direction

Sound design is one of the most important parts of ASTRAVEN.

Do not use constant music.

Use environmental sound:

- machinery
- ventilation
- metal creaks
- wind
- radio interference
- distant impacts
- footsteps
- breathing
- unknown sounds

Combat music should appear when appropriate.

Silence should also be used intentionally.

The player should sometimes hear danger before seeing it.

---

## 11. Music

Music style:

- Dark Ambient
- Sci-Fi Synth
- Retro Synth influence
- Industrial
- Drone
- Low Synth

Music should support atmosphere.

It does not always need to be catchy.

Exploration music should feel mysterious and lonely.

Combat music should increase tension and energy.

---

## 12. Enemy Direction

Enemies are not zombies or ghosts.

Possible enemy origins:

- alien organisms
- parasites
- mutations
- biological weapons
- unknown lifeforms
- infected machines
- cosmic entities

Enemies should have different behaviors.

Avoid simply creating enemies with more HP.

Enemy variety should come from:

- movement
- attack pattern
- speed
- positioning
- reaction to player
- environmental interaction

---

## 13. Boss Design

Bosses must be memorable.

Avoid making bosses difficult only by increasing health.

Bosses should have:

- recognizable silhouette
- unique behavior
- multiple attack patterns
- clear visual identity
- strong connection to environment or story

Possible examples:

- parasite-controlled machine
- giant biological organism
- multi-limbed alien
- dimensional entity
- corrupted research experiment

---

## 14. Storytelling

Avoid long exposition.

Story should mainly be told through:

- environment
- logs
- messages
- abandoned rooms
- broken equipment
- corpses
- research records
- visual clues
- audio
- background events

The player should discover the story gradually.

Do not explain everything immediately.

---

## 15. Example Story Flow

Early game:

1. Land on an unknown planet
2. Investigate a mysterious signal
3. Discover an abandoned station
4. Find signs of recent human activity
5. Encounter unknown lifeforms
6. Escape the station
7. Discover that communication networks are failing
8. Travel to another location searching for survivors
9. Slowly learn that the alien outbreak is widespread

The larger story should expand gradually.

Do not reveal the entire cosmic threat in the first mission.

---

## 16. Level Structure

The game should be divided into missions or chapters.

Initial target:

Each mission approximately 5–10 minutes for early prototype content.

Possible locations:

- Alien Planet Surface
- Abandoned Space Station
- Research Laboratory
- Mining Colony
- Military Base
- Biological Planet
- Derelict Ship
- Orbital Station
- Alien Structure
- Large Mothership

Not every mission needs a boss.

---

## 17. Prototype Goal

The first goal is NOT to build the full game.

The first goal is to build a playable vertical slice.

Prototype must include:

- one playable character
- movement
- jump
- shooting
- one firearm
- one enemy
- health and damage
- one small environment
- one basic encounter
- simple UI
- restart / death flow

Optional after the above works:

- second enemy
- simple boss
- small exploration section
- environmental audio
- basic lighting

The prototype should answer:

> "Is moving, shooting, and exploring this world fun?"

---

## 18. Development Philosophy

ASTRAVEN is a solo-developed project.

AI is used as an assistant.

The goal is to finish a playable game.

Do not over-engineer.

Do not create large systems before they are needed.

Development order:

1. Make it work
2. Make it playable
3. Make it fun
4. Make it look good
5. Expand carefully

Always prefer a small working system over a large unfinished system.

---

## 19. AI Role

AI should act as:

- Technical Lead
- Game Designer
- Code Reviewer
- Debugging Assistant
- Architecture Advisor

AI should:

- review existing code before changing systems
- explain major design decisions
- suggest improvements
- identify risks
- prevent scope creep
- keep systems modular
- prioritize completion

AI should NOT:

- generate the entire game blindly
- add major systems without discussion
- rewrite stable systems without reason
- create unnecessary architecture
- expand scope without approval

---

## 20. Current Project Priority

Current priority:

**Build the first playable prototype as fast as possible.**

Immediate development focus:

1. Godot project setup
2. Player movement
3. Jump
4. Basic camera
5. Shooting
6. One realistic firearm
7. One enemy
8. Damage system
9. Small playable room
10. Test and review

Everything else comes later.
